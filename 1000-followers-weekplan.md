## 2. Positioning Diagnosis — What Roy Is Known For This Week

You are **not** "an AI guy" and **not** "a growth guy." Both lanes are
saturated by 100k+ accounts. Your wedge is the **seam between them**:

> **The architect who's learning to build — and is engineering his own
> distribution as a system, out loud.**

That sentence is your filter. Every post and reply this week should be
recognisably from *that* person. Five pillars, in priority order:

1. **AI-assisted builder** — you ship real product with AI, and you're
   specific about *how* (tools, workflow, what broke). This is your proof.
2. **Architect learning to build** — the transformation arc. You bring 15+
   years of systems thinking to a thing you're new at. That tension is
   relatable to thousands of senior people watching AI eat the gap between
   "designing" and "doing."
3. **X growth as a system** — you don't "do content," you built **X Reach
   Lab** to turn the feed into a workflow. You growth-hack *visibly and
   methodically*, which is rare and credible.
4. **Product taste + build-in-public lessons** — judgment calls, what you
   cut, what you kept, what the AI got wrong. Taste is the moat AI can't
   copy.
5. **Honest growth** — you say out loud that you want 1000 followers this
   week. Milestones matter and pretending they don't is fake-guru energy.
   Owning the number *is* your differentiation.

**Why this works under the algorithm (per the repo doc):** a coherent niche
means the people who engage with you have coherent histories, so the
two-tower retrieval keeps surfacing you to a *relevant* audience that
actually converts to follows (§3.2). A scattered "everything" account
dilutes that signal and earns `not_interested` from mismatched viewers
(negative weight).

**What to drop this week:** generic motivation, "10 lessons" listicles with
no proof, hot takes outside your lane, anything you can't back with a real
artifact from your own build.

| Float | anytime | ~5 | Reply to anyone who replied to *you* — within minutes |

**Time budget: ~90 minutes total/day across blocks.** That fits a builder
with a job and family. Quality over essay-length — see below.

### What to reply to

- Posts **< 30 minutes old** from T1 (you want to be in the first 10–15
  replies, where you're actually seen).
- Posts asking a **question** or making a **claim you can extend or
  respectfully challenge** with a real example from your build.
- Posts in **your lane**: AI building, solo founder reality, X
  distribution, product taste, the design→build transition.
- **Anything someone replied to you with** — fastest profile-visit-to-follow
  path there is.

### What NOT to reply to

- Pure news/announcements where you'd just say "congrats 🔥" (adds nothing,
  no profile visit).
- Politics, drama, dunks, anything off-lane — earns `not_interested` from
  your real audience and muddies retrieval.
- Posts already at 200+ replies unless you have a genuinely sharp angle
  (you'll be buried).
- "Great post!" / "This 💯" / emoji-only — invisible, zero conversion.
- Threads from accounts you have **no credible angle** into.

### Strong Roy-style replies (use these as templates)

> **On a big AI-coding post about a shiny new feature:**
> "Architect brain says the interesting part isn't the feature, it's what it
> deletes from the workflow. I rebuilt [X] in Reach Lab last week and the win
> wasn't speed — it was that I stopped context-switching. Did you see the
> same, or just faster?"

> **On a build-in-public 'shipped it' post:**
> "Congrats — what did you *cut* to ship this? That's the decision I keep
> getting wrong. I keep over-architecting the v1." *(specific, invites a
> reply, shows your identity)*

> **On an X-growth post:**
> "I'm treating my own distribution as a system this week — replies as
> retrieval, posts as conversion. Trying to go 659 → 1000 in 7 days as the
> test case. Counter-intuitive thing so far: the peer replies outperform the
> big-account replies for actual follows. Matches what you said?"

> **On a design-taste post (as the architect):**
> "I can spot a bad system in 5 seconds and a bad UI not at all — retraining
> that muscle right now. This is the kind of detail I'd have shipped over.
> What's the tell you use?"

### Weak replies to avoid

- "Great thread 🙌" — invisible.
- "So true, I needed this today 🙏" — guru-coded, no angle.
- "Check out my product 👉 [link]" — reads as spam, can earn block/mute
  (negative weight).
- A 6-line essay nobody asked for — burns your time, rarely beats one sharp
  line.
- Agreeing with everything — disagreement-with-substance gets more profile
  visits than praise.

**Conversion habit:** every reply that gets a like or reply back → check
that person's profile, and if they're in-lane, reply to one more of their
posts the same day. That's how a reply becomes a relationship becomes a
follow.

## 6. Engagement-Farming Rules

These formats are **allowed and encouraged** — they directly farm `reply`, a
named model output. But they're a tool, not the identity.

### Acceptable formats
- **Wrong answers only** ("Wrong answers only: secret to X growth?")
- **"Which one?"** binary ("Ship rough vs polish — which grows you faster?")
- **"What are you building?"** (relationship gold — you reply to all)
- **"Drop your product"** (goodwill + you genuinely look)
- **Hot take** (contrarian, in-lane)
- **Simple binary questions** (low friction = high reply count)
- **Founder-pain questions** ("what quietly drains you most?")

### Guardrails (non-negotiable)
1. **≥50% of bait posts must tie back to building, AI, X, products, or
   systems.** "Drop your product" and "what are you building" qualify;
   "best pizza topping" does not. Off-lane bait pulls off-lane followers who
   never engage again and pollutes your retrieval signal.
2. **No pure empty bait.** Every bait post should still sound like *Roy* —
   add a one-line opinion or your own answer so it's not a hollow hook.
3. **Reply to every good answer, fast.** A bait post you don't work is half a
   post. Budget 15–20 min after each bait post to reply to responders.
4. **Turn replies into relationships.** Pick the 3–5 best responders, visit
   their profile, reply to one of *their* posts. That's the conversion.
5. **Max ~1 pure-bait post/day.** More than that and your timeline reads as a
   game-show host, which repels the senior/architect audience you actually
   want.

## 7. Conversion Plan — Attention → Followers

A profile visit is wasted if the profile doesn't close. Lock these:

- **Profile stays crystal clear.** Bio already nails it ("design systems →
  build them, AI-assisted products, building X Reach Lab in public"). Don't
  touch it mid-week — consistency is the signal. Banner stays.
- **Pin works hard.** Your AI-assisted-coding transformation post is a good
  pin. **Consider swapping it Day 1** to the **"659 → 1000 in 7 days" mission
  post** — a live, specific, honest goal is a stronger profile-visit-to-follow
  hook this week than an evergreen story. Swap back after the sprint.
- **Reply under your own posts to add context.** First reply on every S4
  post = the proof/number/screenshot you didn't fit in the post. Keeps the
  conversation in-network and gives latecomers a reason to follow.
- **DM people who show strong interest.** Anyone who replies thoughtfully
  twice, or asks about X Reach Lab → short, human DM: "Appreciated your take
  on [X] — what are you building?" No pitch. Relationships, not funnels.
- **Ask specific follow-up questions.** In replies and DMs: specifics get
  answers, answers get profile visits. "What broke?" beats "cool!"
- **Collect X Reach Lab feedback.** Every interested person is a potential
  early user. Keep a running note of who's curious; the proof-of-build posts
  double as a soft waitlist. ("Building this for exactly this problem — want
  early access?")

**The chain to keep front of mind (per repo doc §3.5):**
post/reply → profile_click → follow_author. Posts and replies earn the
click; **the profile earns the follow.** Don't optimise one and neglect the
other.
